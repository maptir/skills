# Gacha, live-service, gear-chase

## Where the irreversible decisions hide

This genre breaks the assumption the rest of this skill runs on. There is no
fixed total to find. The scarce thing is **income gated by the calendar** and
**windows that close**, so the budget is a rate, not a number, and every
recommendation has to be dated.

- **Pull currency.** Free income per patch cycle is effectively fixed. Spending
  it on the wrong banner is permanently gone, and the loss is invisible because
  nothing tells you what you gave up.
- **Pity and guarantee state.** Whether progress carries between banners, what
  a 50/50 costs when lost, where soft pity starts. Every "should I pull" answer
  depends on a number the guide cannot see, which changes what the guide should
  say — see the traps below.
- **Banner windows.** A unit is available for a few weeks, then gone for a year
  or forever. The hardest deadline in any genre, and the reason the calendar is
  the spine here rather than a route.
- **One-time-only choices.** Launch and anniversary selectors, "pick any 5-star"
  tickets, redemption codes with expiry dates, starter bundles. Choosing wrong
  is permanent and usually happens in the first hour, before the player knows
  anything.
- **Materials sunk into a unit they later drop.** Most of these games refund
  nothing, some refund partially. Find out which, because it decides whether the
  guide says "commit" or "experiment".
- **Account-level locks.** Region or server (usually no transfer), account
  binding, display name. All decided before the player understands them.
- **The reroll window.** Exists only before binding. If the game has a real
  reroll culture, saying so on day one is worth more than any build advice.
- **Daily and weekly caps.** Income not collected today is gone — unlike a
  single-player game, where the resource waits. This is the genre's true
  scarcity and the thing new players underestimate most.
- **Expiring event currency.** Event shops close with the event; leftovers do
  not convert.

## Numbers to hunt

Free currency income per patch cycle · pity count, soft-pity threshold, 50/50
rules and whether a loss carries · full cost to build one unit (levels, skills,
gear, weapon) · daily and weekly caps and how many days to reach them · historic
rerun frequency for older units · event currency total versus the shop's full
cost · how much of the content is actually cleared by a beginner roster.

The comparison that carries the guide: **income per cycle versus the cost of one
guaranteed unit.** Everything downstream — save or pull, which banner, whether a
weapon banner is ever worth it — follows from that single ratio.

## Tabs that usually earn their place

Before you start, which is unusually heavy here (region, binding, reroll window,
active codes, what the selectors mean) · The pull budget: income per cycle
against a guarantee · Pity rules, stated plainly, with the numbers · Banner and
event calendar — the timing spine · Who to build first with what you already
have · The daily routine, and which parts are safe to skip · Event shop
priority · What never to spend on.

## Advice traps

- **Tier lists rot faster here than anywhere.** Power creep ships monthly. If
  you give one, date it, name the patch, and say what content it assumes.
- **"Save for X" is not advice without their pity count.** Teach the decision
  rule — how far from a guarantee, how much income before the banner closes,
  what the roster already lacks — so it survives a state you cannot see.
- **Never recommend spending money.** The guide works free-to-play, and can then
  say where money has the best rate if they choose to spend. Recommending a
  purchase is the one thing that turns a guide into marketing.
- **Separate meta from necessary.** Most of the content clears with almost any
  roster. Saying which fights actually need investment, and that the rest do
  not, is the most useful sentence in the guide.
- **FOMO is the genre's monetisation mechanic, so do not amplify it.** A guide
  that treats every banner as unmissable is doing the publisher's work. State
  plainly what is genuinely one-time and what reruns, because most of it reruns.
- **Dailies are a habit trap.** Say which are worth the minutes and which the
  player can drop without falling behind, and say it early.
- **Date every number.** These figures expire. Put the patch and the date on the
  page, and say when the guide will need a refresh.
