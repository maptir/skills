# RPG / JRPG / CRPG / action-RPG

## Where the irreversible decisions hide

- **Stat and level spending.** Levels are usually one-way, or respec is limited
  to a countable number of items. Find out which, and how many respecs exist —
  that number changes whether the guide should say "commit" or "experiment".
- **Upgrade materials with a fixed total.** The classic: a rare material that
  fully upgrades only two or three pieces of gear per run. Get the total, get
  the cost curve, then state plainly how many things the player can max.
- **Exclusive trades.** An item exchanged for one of two rewards; a merchant
  whose stock is finite; a faction that locks its rival's rewards. Every one of
  these is a tab-worthy fork.
- **Companion and faction commitments.** Recruiting one often forecloses
  another, and the loss is invisible until much later.
- **NPC questlines with no quest log.** Chains that advance when the world
  advances — a boss dies, an area opens — and break silently, with nothing in
  the UI to tell you. Standard in souls-likes and common across the genre. Run
  the sweep in SKILL.md §3c on every RPG; in these games this one category
  produces more permanent loss than all the others together.
- **Chapter-gated content.** Side content that disappears when the area's story
  advances. This is the single largest source of "I missed it" in the genre.

## Numbers to hunt

Total count of each upgrade material and how much a full upgrade costs · number
of respec items · skill/talent points available across a full run versus points
needed to fill a tree · gear slot counts and when each unlocks · vendor stock
limits · which stats have diminishing returns and roughly where.

The comparison that carries the guide: **what a full build costs versus what the
game actually gives you.** If the tree needs 56 points and the game grants 25,
say so in the first tab — every later recommendation follows from it.

## Tabs that usually earn their place

The loop and the budget · Build options (one per viable stat path, with real trade-offs) ·
The main progression tree with a spend order · Gear and upgrade budget ·
Exclusive trades and forks · Companions/factions if they exist · Missables by
chapter · Collection route.

## Advice traps

- **Do not crown one build.** Present two or three that genuinely work and say
  what each feels like to play. Players who follow a single blessed build and
  bounce off its playstyle blame the guide.
- **Scaling grades are not damage.** A weapon with A-scaling can lose to a
  B-scaling weapon with higher base damage. Say what a grade means in context
  rather than treating letters as a ranking.
- **Separate "strong" from "strong for a first run".** The optimal endgame setup
  is often unusable for the first two-thirds of the game. Order recommendations
  by when they become available, not by ceiling.
- **Respec changes the tone of everything.** If respec is cheap and unlimited,
  the guide should encourage experimentation rather than lay down a rigid order.
  Check before writing.
