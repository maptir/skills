# Management, city-builder, 4X, colony sim

## Where the irreversible decisions hide

These games rarely have "missable items". Their irreversibility is structural:

- **Layout you cannot undo cheaply.** A first district, a starting base, a rail
  spine. Bad early geometry compounds for the whole run because everything later
  is built around it.
- **Opening build order.** The first ten to thirty minutes determine whether the
  economy compounds or stalls. This is the genre's equivalent of a build.
- **Tech and civic choices** that fork the tree or are one-per-run.
- **Difficulty and world-generation settings** chosen at the start and locked
  for the entire save — often the single biggest determinant of how the run goes,
  and the one most guides skip.
- **Death spirals.** A resource that, once it goes negative, gets harder to fix
  the longer it stays negative. Naming these early is more valuable than any
  optimisation tip.

## Numbers to hunt

Starting resources and burn rate · the ratio the economy actually turns on (food
per worker, power per building, upkeep per unit) · tech costs and how they scale
· population or size thresholds that unlock or break things · the point where
difficulty steps up (raids, winter, demand spikes) and how long you have to
prepare.

## Tabs that usually earn their place

The loop and the budget (plus the failure modes) · Settings and world setup, because
it is decided once and forever · Opening build order with timings · The core
ratio, explained with a worked example · Layout patterns worth copying, drawn ·
Threat timeline — what arrives when, and what you need ready · Tech priorities ·
Recovery: what to do when a spiral has already started.

## Advice traps

- **Timings beat lists.** "Have X by minute 20" is actionable; "X is important"
  is not. Prefer thresholds and deadlines.
- **Do not prescribe a blueprint grid without the reasoning.** Players adapt to
  the map they rolled. Teach the constraint the layout satisfies — throughput,
  walking distance, coverage radius — so they can regenerate it themselves.
- **Say what a stall looks like early.** Most players' first run dies to a
  problem that had visible symptoms twenty minutes before it became fatal.
- **Separate the sim from the meta.** Some systems reward optimisation and some
  are noise. Saying "ignore this system for your first run" is genuinely useful
  and rarely said.
